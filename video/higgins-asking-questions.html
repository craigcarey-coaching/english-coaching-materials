Who is speaking, and why?

Michael D. Higgins was President of Ireland from 2011 to 2025. Before that, he had a long career as a university lecturer in sociology and politics in Galway, a Labour Party politician, and Ireland’s Minister for Arts and Culture. He is also a published poet.

In Ireland, the President is the head of state, but the job has very little political power. It is mostly symbolic. This gives the President freedom to talk about big ideas — and Higgins used that freedom a lot. He spoke often about ethics, education, and what kind of society Ireland should become.

In 2017 he helped to launch the Irish Young Philosopher Awards, a competition where school pupils, from about age nine to eighteen, send in projects that explore big questions. He wanted young people to learn to think critically, not just to prepare for a job.

In June 2021, in the middle of the COVID-19 pandemic, he opened the fourth year of the awards. For the first time there was also an international prize, with students from seven other countries. Its theme was solidarity.

6

Let’s talk about it

“We are sleep-walking.”

Michael D. Higgins, 2021. What do you think he means? Do you agree?

Start here

  • Did the pandemic make you question anything about your work or your way of life? What changed, and what went back to normal?
  • Higgins says we should not need a crisis to start asking big questions. Why do you think people and societies usually wait?
  • What does solidarity mean to you? Describe a moment when you saw it, or felt it.

Go deeper

  • He says work can be a way to express yourself, a necessity, and a way to survive. Which of these describes your work best? Has that changed over time?
  • Do companies in your country put profit before their employees’ family and community life? Can you give an example?
  • Should philosophy be a school subject? From what age? What would it replace?
  • He wants people who ask “awkward questions” in workplaces and in government. Who asks awkward questions where you work — and what happens to them?

Make it personal

  • In which part of your life are you an active shaper, and in which are you more of a passive observer?
  • What is a question you think everyone should ask themselves at least once a year?
  • Higgins was speaking to teenagers. If you could give one piece of advice to your 16-year-old self about asking questions, what would it be?

Language to try

Giving an opinion

  • As I see it, …
  • I’d argue that …
  • To be honest, I think …

Agreeing partly

  • I see what he means, but …
  • That’s true up to a point.
  • It depends what we mean by …

Disagreeing politely

  • I’m not sure I agree.
  • I’d question that slightly.
  • Another way to look at it is …

Buying time

  • That’s a difficult one.
  • Let me think about that for a second.
  • I’ve never really thought about it, but …
