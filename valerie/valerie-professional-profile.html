My professional profile

Where you've been, and where you're heading — in your own words, built one block at a time.

Five short parts. You don't have to write them in order, and no single part needs to be long. Write a little in each, and the whole thing assembles itself below.

There are three structures to land — tick each one when you spot it in your writing. That's the target: not "was it good enough", just "did I use all three?" Land the three and the profile is complete.

Say each sentence out loud before you type it. It comes out better that way — it always does.

Three to land

Must use
Present perfect · since / for — lands in Part 1
How long you've done what you do. The bridge between your past and now.
"I've worked in health-and-safety compliance for over twenty years."
Past simple + past perfect — lands in Part 2
What you did, and what you had already built before you moved on.
"I led the site audits. By the time I left, I had built a system the team relied on."
Concession / contrast — lands in Part 3
The turn. You honour where you came from and still point forward.
"Although my background is in compliance, I'm moving toward the people side of the work."
Your profile will assemble here as you write — read it as one piece when you're done.
0 words Read it aloud, start to finish, before you walk it through.

Walk it through

When you present it, don't read it twice — point to the parts.

  1. Read the whole profile aloud, once, without stopping.
  2. Point to your since / for — how long have you done your work?
  3. Point to your past perfect — what had you built before you left?
  4. Point to your concession — where does the direction turn?
  5. One line you'd like to make stronger next time. (Just one — and only what you'd add, not what's wrong.)
For when the three feel easy — sharper ways to make the turn.
Front the concession for weight
"Compliance-trained though I am, I've come to care more about the people than the paperwork."
Moving though to the front makes the contrast land harder than a plain "although".
Concede a whole clause, then pivot
"For all the years I spent enforcing rules, what I'll miss least is the enforcing."
"For all (that)…" concedes generously before the turn — confident, not defensive.
Contrast your two selves
"Where I once measured risk, I now want to reduce it for the people carrying it."
"Where I once…, I now…" makes the before/after the sentence's own shape.
