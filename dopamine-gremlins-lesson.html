Model summary (92 words)

In this segment, Dr Anna Lembke explains why digital habits can become compulsive. She describes the brain as a balance that tries to keep pleasure and pain level. Each scroll or notification gives us a small hit of dopamine, which tips the balance towards pleasure. The brain then compensates by adding weight to the pain side, her "gremlins", so we feel a come-down and a craving for more. With repeated use, the gremlins stay, tolerance builds, and we can end up in a dopamine deficit, needing the device just to feel normal.

C1 compressions (models)

Casual: Basically, every scroll gives you a buzz, your brain pays it back with a low, and you end up needing your phone just to feel OK.

Formal: Lembke contends that repeated digital stimulation disrupts the brain's pleasure–pain equilibrium. Compensatory adaptation produces tolerance and, ultimately, a dopamine-deficit state in which continued use is required merely to restore baseline mood.
