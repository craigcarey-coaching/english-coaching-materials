Nuclear Waste, Nuclear Futures

Listening, discussion and register practice built around a TEDx talk by nuclear safety engineer Jo Lackenby. She argues that nuclear waste is not a problem to fear but a resource for the future. You don’t have to agree.

Part 1: The reputation problem

Stance, concession, and saying one idea at three levels of formality.

Before you watch

  • When you tell someone at a dinner what you do for a living, what reactions do you get? Which one annoys you most?
  • The speaker is a nuclear safety engineer who calls herself an environmentalist. Predict the three arguments she will make. Which do you expect to be the weakest?

The dinner-party problem

0:11 to 2:56, about 2 min 45 s

While you watch, note one point where you agree with the speaker and one where you would push back.

After you watch

  • Did she make the arguments you predicted?
  • She suggests that nuclear isn’t perfect, but neither is any other energy source. Is that a strong argument or a deflection?
  • She treats public fear as the main obstacle to nuclear. Is it? What would you rank above it?
  • How does the public conversation about nuclear in your country compare with other places you know?

Evaluating an argument, and reporting verbs that show what you think of a claim.

Before you watch

  • Does the label matter legally? What changes if something is classified one way rather than the other?

The ask

Discuss

  • What does the talk leave out that a regulator or a sanctions lawyer would raise first?
  • If a recycling future depends on technology concentrated in a few countries, is that a strategic risk?
  • Is “nuclear waste is a resource” a fact, a prediction, or a policy choice?
