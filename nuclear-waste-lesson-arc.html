Why this talk works

Lackenby is a nuclear safety engineer making an advocacy case to a lay audience, and she does it in a deliberately chatty register: dinner-party anecdotes, a fireplace metaphor, words like icky and utopia. That gives you two levers. First, a lawyer who works in a compliance-heavy corner of this industry knows the terrain far better than the audience the talk was written for, so the natural task is to evaluate an advocate’s case rather than to comprehend it. Second, the talk’s informal language is ideal raw material for register work: every casual line can be pushed up into the neutral-professional and formal-legal registers she actually works in.

Each session carries one named pattern. Session 1 is the Register Ladder (one idea, three heights). Session 2 is Reporting Verbs that carry judgement (the verb you choose to report a claim signals whether you accept it). Both are elicited from the learner before any model language is shown.

Optional Session 3

If the arc is going well, a third class can use the reserve clip (3:49 to 5:06) for the language of data and risk: comparing rates, describing outliers, and qualifying statistics (deaths per unit of energy, a single event dominating a dataset). Open with the Session 2 Bridge Task, then have her challenge how the chart is used, which brings reporting verbs back without re-teaching them.
