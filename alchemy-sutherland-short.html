The logic of magic

Rory Sutherland, of the advertising agency Ogilvy, argues that people don't behave logically, so the best business ideas often don't look logical either. His 2019 book is called Alchemy: creating value by changing how people see things, not the things themselves.

Language from the talk

8 min

Phrasal verbs and idioms · tap a time to hear it

  • 2:41take the hit — accept a loss or cost. We took the hit on margins to keep the client.
  • 3:08put back — return something to its place. Please put the files back when you've finished.
  • 11:13get rid of — remove. The new system let us get rid of two manual steps.
  • 12:19do your head in — confuse or frustrate you (informal, British). Three different spreadsheets? That does my head in.
  • 16:51hit pay dirt — suddenly find something very valuable. After ten failed tests, we finally hit pay dirt.
  • 23:25take on board — accept and act on information. We've taken your feedback on board.
  • 3:02C1sidle up to — approach someone quietly, a little secretly. He sidled up to the director after the meeting.
  • 24:08C1trapped in a local maximum — stuck at "good enough", unable to find something better. Cost-cutting alone kept us trapped in a local maximum.

Grammar 1 · Reporting what a speaker says

To summarise a talk, use a reporting verb + that. In summaries we usually keep the present tense: Sutherland argues that…, He points out that…, He suggests that…. For a single past event, use the past: He said that usage went up four times on the first day.

Grammar 2 · Agreeing, but not completely

To give a balanced view, accept one point, then limit it. Although / While + clause, main clause: While better Wi-Fi is cheaper, some passengers really need speed. Or two sentences: He has a point. However, …. Note: despite + noun, not + clause: Despite the cost, …

C1Granted, faster trains are useful; even so, … · Much as I admire his examples, …

Summarise and respond

10 min

1 · Summarise in three sentences

Say it to a partner first, then write it. Give one sentence per clip and use a reporting verb in each.

2 · Where do you stand?

Now write one sentence where you agree with Sutherland and one where you don't. Use while, although or however.

Upgrade point

Churchill presents himself as a "fellow offender rather than an accuser". This is a good moment for defuse a situation (désamorcer). Many write-ups wrongly say "diffuse".
