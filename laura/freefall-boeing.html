10:10 · input

The story, in one breath

Read this first. Then underline eight to ten words you'd call industry words — before we sort them into families together.

In 2009, Boeing opened a plant in North Charleston to build its new 787 "Dreamliner" faster and more cheaply. Much of the aircraft's structure was outsourced to suppliers around the world and joined together on site. Freefall follows the quality inspectors who raised the alarm. Chief among them was John Barnett, who spent 32 years at Boeing before reporting to the regulator that production was too flawed to be safe — gaps forced shut, metal shavings left near wiring, emergency oxygen systems that failed in testing. He accused the company of retaliation and died in 2024. The film argues that a management culture obsessed with shareholder value put profit ahead of safety, and traces the same logic from the Dreamliner to the 737 MAX and the door plug that blew out of an Alaska Airlines jet in January 2024. ▶ Warm-up option: the 2m19s trailer works as a listening clip before you read.
the lexicon

Five families of industry English

Not a list to memorise — collocations and register. The red-edged cards are false friends: the word looks English but means something else in French.

1 · Airframe & manufacturing
You may know these better than I do — teach me.
fuselage
the main body of the aircraft — the tube.
"holes in the aft fuselage sections"
aft / forward
toward the rear / toward the nose.
"the aft section" (not "back section")
airframe
the mechanical structure — everything but the engines.
"airframe integrity"
tolerances
the permitted margin of error in a part.
"parts must meet tight tolerances"
to shim / a gap
a thin spacer used to fill a gap between parts.
"gaps were shimmed to hide them"
structural integrity
how sound and reliable the structure is.
"it threatened the structural integrity"
2 · Quality & oversight
quality control / assurance
checking the output (QC) vs building quality into the process (QA).
"a quality inspector on the line"
to conduct an audit
a formal, systematic check.
"the FAA conducted an audit of the plant"
oversight
supervision / monitoring by an authority.
"regulatory oversight was too light"
airworthiness / to certify
officially cleared as safe to fly.
"the type was certified as airworthy"
control
un contrôle = a check / an inspection. English "control" = command or power.
✗ "I did a control" → ✓ "I ran a check / carried out an inspection"
assurance
only "self-assurance" or "quality assurance". assurance (money) = insurance.
✗ "an assurance policy" → ✓ "an insurance policy"
3 · Safety & failure
safety vs security
sécurité is both. Safety = preventing accidents. Security = preventing threats/attacks.
"a safety failure" (not a security failure)
defect · flaw · fault
a fault in a part or design.
"a manufacturing defect"
root cause / corrective action
the underlying reason / the fix that follows.
"identify the root cause"
redundancy / fail-safe
backup systems / designed to fail into a safe state.
"built-in redundancy"
to ground a fleet
to stop aircraft from flying.
"regulators grounded the fleet"
C1
safety-critical
a part/system whose failure endangers the flight.
"a safety-critical component"
4 · Corporate & economic
to outsource / a supplier
to have work done by an outside company.
"structure outsourced to cheaper suppliers"
supply chain
the network of suppliers feeding production.
"a fragile supply chain"
to cut corners
to skip proper steps to save time or money.
"under pressure, they cut corners"
shareholder value
returns to owners of the stock.
"putting shareholder value first"
cost-cutting / margins
reducing spending / profit per unit.
"relentless cost-cutting"
C1
a race to the bottom
competition that erodes standards.
"outsourcing became a race to the bottom"
5 · Accountability & law
Your home turf — the vocabulary maps straight onto litigation.
a whistleblower / to blow the whistle
someone who reports wrongdoing inside an organisation.
"he blew the whistle on the plant"
a deposition / to be deposed
pre-trial sworn questioning by lawyers.
"he died during a break in his deposition"
liability / negligence
legal responsibility / failure of a duty of care.
"exposed to huge liability"
retaliation / to retaliate
punishing someone for speaking up.
"he sued for retaliation"
evidence
évidence = obviousness. English evidence = preuve(s).
✗ "it's an evidence" → ✓ "it's obvious" / "the evidence shows"
to report
reporter = to postpone. To report = to signal / un rapport.
✗ "report the meeting to Friday" → ✓ "postpone the meeting"
to prevent
prévenir = to warn / notify. Prevent = empêcher.
✗ "prevent the FAA" → ✓ "warn the FAA"
deception
déception = disappointment. Deception = tromperie.
✗ "a big deception for staff" → ✓ "a big disappointment"
C1
regulatory capture
when a regulator serves the industry it should police.
"critics allege regulatory capture"

Now write your own analysis

Pick one prompt. Say your opening sentence aloud before you type it. The contract below is your finish line — not "is it good", but "did I do these five things".

A · Argument
Aviation regulators should tighten safety oversight of manufacturers, even if it slows production and raises prices. Do you agree?
180–220 words · take a clear position
B · Briefing note
Brief a colleague who hasn't seen Freefall: what does it reveal about Boeing's safety culture, and why does it matter?
~150 words · neutral, informative register
Must-use contract — tick before you start, audit when you finish

Teacher key & notes

Collocation answers

  • blow → the whistle
  • raise → concerns / the alarm
  • meet → tolerances / standards
  • cut → corners
  • ground → a fleet
  • conduct → an audit
  • build → trust
  • put profits ahead of → safety

False-friend repairs (models)

  • "Boeing did a control on each plane." → ran a check / carried out an inspection
  • "It's an evidence that safety came second." → It's obvious that… / The evidence shows…
  • "Barnett wanted to prevent the FAA." → warn / notify the FAA
  • "They reported the hearing to next month." → postponed the hearing
  • "There was a big deception among staff." → disappointment
  • "They must achieve the inspection before delivery." → complete / finish the inspection

Teaching moves

  • Expert seat. Family 1 is hers — let her supply the airframe terms, you supply the collocations and register. She's the pilot; you're the language guide.
  • Discovery before naming. She underlines "industry words" in the spine before the families appear. Name the concession clause only after she's noticed the model does it — then link it to legal argument (concede, then rebut).
  • Speak-then-write at both scales: the spoken one-line thesis at the bridge becomes her opening sentence; each repaired sentence is said aloud first.
  • Feedback as upgrades. Two precision lifts (a collocation + a false friend) and one structural nudge. No more. She's strong — be honest, stay in upgrade framing.
  • Harvest. Pull 3–4 French-influenced phrasings for her reformulation sheet; the traps can feed the aviation faux-amis glossary next time you report entries.
  • Sits beside, not on top of, the 737 MAX listening lesson — this is the 787 / quality-culture angle with written production as the target.
  • Accuracy in the room: the film is broader than the Dreamliner (737 MAX + the Alaska door plug too). If she's watched it, that's your way in.

Closing recap (non-negotiable)

  • She leaves saying 3 words + 1 structure she's taking with her. Bridge to lesson 2.
