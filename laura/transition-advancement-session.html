The offer

14 min

Around half the world's commercial aircraft are owned and financed out of Dublin, so the vocabulary below sits at the join between your field and finance. Ten expressions are underlined; work out each one before you tap it.

From: Aoife Brennan, Head of Talent · Liffey Aviation Capital, Dublin
Subject: Offer of employment — Junior Analyst, Portfolio Strategy

Dear Laura,

Further to your interviews last week, I'm delighted to confirm our offer for the position of Junior Analyst, Portfolio Strategy, reporting to our Head of Fleet Risk.

The starting salary is €48,000 per annum, paid monthly . You will also be eligible for a bonus of up to 12%, in your first year.

gross = before tax and deductions (FR brut). The opposite is net — what reaches your account. The two figures are far apart.
in arrears = paid at the end of the period worked, not the start. The first payment arrives after a month of work.
discretionary = decided by the company; not guaranteed and not contractual. "Up to 12%" includes 0%. Compare with a guaranteed or contractual bonus.
pro-rated = scaled to the time actually worked. Starting in October gives roughly a quarter of it. Verb: to pro-rate.

The first six months constitute a , during which either party may terminate with two weeks' .

probation period = FR période d'essai. In everyday English, probation on its own usually refers to a criminal sentence served outside prison, so keep the full phrase or say trial period.
notice = the warning period before leaving (FR préavis). Collocations: to give notice, to work your notice, a three-month notice period. Not prevent — prévenir is to let someone know or to warn.

You will be in our pension scheme, where we match your contributions up to 5%, and in the company health plan, which is treated as a for tax purposes. Annual leave is 25 days, public holidays.

enrolled = signed up, registered (FR inscrit). To enrol in a scheme, a course, a programme. British spelling doubles the L in enrolled.
benefit-in-kind = FR avantage en nature. Something of value that isn't cash — health cover, a company car — which the tax authority still counts as income. Note that scheme and plan are neutral words here.
exclusive of = not counted in, so the 25 days come in addition to the public holidays. It looks like exclusion; it means addition. Opposite: inclusive of.

A relocation allowance of €3,000 is available on production of receipts. The offer is satisfactory references and proof of your right to work in Ireland.

subject to = conditional on; the offer can still fall through (FR sous réserve de). An Irish passport settles the second condition immediately.

We'd be grateful if you could revert by Friday. Do come back to me with any questions in the meantime.

Kind regards,
Aoife

Reading the detail

  1. Is the bonus money you can count on?
    No. Discretionary and up to both allow zero. It shouldn't be budgeted for.
  2. You contribute 3% to the pension. What goes in in total?
    6% — they match up to 5%, so they match your 3%. Contributing 5% would put 10% in.
  3. How many days of leave do you actually get?
    25 plus the public holidays — around 34 in Ireland. Exclusive of adds rather than subtracts.
  4. Do you receive €3,000 for the move?
    Only against receipts, and only up to €3,000. On production of = when you show us.
  5. Say the monthly gross figure out loud.
    €4,000 — "four thousand euro a month, before tax". Then the annual: "forty-eight thousand".
  6. Name three questions worth asking before signing.
    Possible lines: is the bonus formula published? what happens to the pension contributions if you leave in year one? does the health cover extend to a partner?
  7. What does revert mean here, and would you use it yourself?
    "Get back to me." It is business-letter English, common in Ireland and India, and stiff elsewhere. Worth understanding; write get back to you instead.
