Who sits at the table

Mary Robinson on parity in leadership, and the language you need to discuss it in front of a Cambridge panel. A lesson for Milda.

1

The argument in brief

Read this after the clip. The underlined words return later in the lesson.

Mary Robinson, former President of Ireland and former UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, argues that the world has been governed largely by men, and that this imbalance has consequences. Her claim is not that women are inherently better leaders, but that leadership needs a balance of perspectives.

Male-dominated leadership, she suggests, has tended to be hierarchical and to favour hard power. Women who have risen through grassroots networks tend to lead in a more collaborative way. Real parity at the top, in her view, could produce a less militarised, more cooperative world.

She also argues that gender equality has too often been paid lip service, criticises double standards in how international law is applied, and insists that states be held to account. She makes the case for a woman as the next UN Secretary-General.

Retell it twice. First, tell a friend over coffee what she said, in two sentences.
Then summarise it for a fellowship panel, in two sentences. What changed in your vocabulary, your sentence length, and how certain you sounded?
